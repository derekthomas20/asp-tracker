Biosimilar net cost recovery

Compare what each product in a market recovers after acquisition cost, per billing unit and per administered dose. Or build your own comparison from any Part B drug in the CMS crosswalk: brands against each other, or a biosimilar market against newer products.

    What counts as a dose?

    One administration to one patient at one visit. Per kg and per m² doses are the dose times the patient’s weight or body surface area; a flat dose is the same for everyone. The amount is rounded up to whole billing units. “Per dose” results are for one administration, and “doses given per year” counts administrations across all your patients.

    How the numbers are calculated
    Billing units / package = package strength ÷ HCPCS unit Net cost / unit = contract price × (1 − rebate %) ÷ billing units Paid / unit = payment limit × (1 + limit change) × [Medicare share × (1 − 0.8 × 2% if sequestration) + commercial share × commercial %] NCR / unit = paid / unit − net cost / unit NCR / dose = paid / unit × billed units − acquisition cost of the vials used With 340B on (340B share s): Package cost = (1 − s) × other price × (1 − rebate %) + s × 340B price

    Payment limit. Each product’s per-unit payment limit is prefilled from the CMS {{ latest_q|quarter }} quarterly file by its HCPCS code. For a reference product it is ASP + 6%. For a qualifying biosimilar it is the biosimilar’s ASP plus 8% of the reference product’s ASP, so a biosimilar can pay more per unit than its own price suggests. Confirm each product’s add-on period. Limits reflect sales from about two quarters earlier, so use the limit-change slider to test erosion.

    Doses. Doses round up to whole billing units. Without leftover reuse, multi-dose vials are drawn first so any waste falls on single-dose vials, where JW billing can apply. With reuse, single-dose vials are drawn first and the multi-dose remainder is saved, so only the drug actually used is costed. Vial selection minimizes discarded drug rather than maximizing billable waste.

    Yearly volume. Enter it as doses a year or as billing units administered a year (for example, from your claims records). Billing units are turned into doses by dividing by the billing units in one dose, so the doses a year can be a fraction. In your own comparisons, a product’s own billing units a year are turned into doses the same way with its own dose.

    Packages per year. The vials used per dose times the doses given per year. With multi-dose reuse on, multi-dose vials are counted by the drug actually used, so those counts are approximate (marked ≈).

    340B. Medicare Part B pays the same payment limit whether or not a drug was bought under 340B, so turning 340B on changes only your cost. Each package’s cost becomes a mix of its 340B price and the price you pay for other patients, weighted by the 340B share. The rebate applies only to the non-340B price. When the 340B share is between 0% and 100%, the summary also shows which product leads for 340B doses and for other doses, because they can differ. Some 340B hospitals cannot buy through a group purchasing organization (GPO) for their other patients; enter the price you actually pay.

    Not modeled. Patient coinsurance collection, manufacturer discarded-drug refunds, administration fees, commercial payers that pay differently for 340B drugs, Medicaid duplicate-discount rules, and payer step or preferred-product rules. A payer that requires one product makes NCR moot for those patients.

    Products and packages. Which products compete in each market comes from the site’s biosimilar groups list, checked by hand; it is not a CMS file. Package sizes come from the CMS {{ latest_q|quarter }} NDC–HCPCS crosswalk, which lists the billing units in each package. Whether a vial is single- or multi-dose and the default doses come from the FDA labels and can be changed. Example prices are made up for illustration and are not real contracts; there are no example 340B prices.

    Your own comparisons. You can compare any products in the CMS {{ latest_q|quarter }} NDC–HCPCS crosswalk, one product per HCPCS code and crosswalk drug name, with its payment limit and billing unit from the CMS file. CMS files don’t give doses, so enter each product’s dose yourself; a product that is also in one of the markets above starts with that market’s dose, packages and vial types. Every other product’s packages start as single-dose vials: set multi-dose vials, or mark a package Not used, yourself. Because products can have different billing units and doses, they are ranked per dose, and NCR per unit is only highlighted when every included product has the same billing unit. Each product can have its own doses (or billing units) per year, for products given on different schedules; left blank, it uses the figure under Market, dose and payer. Products are ranked by NCR per year, which is the same order as per dose when every product has the same doses per year. The breakeven price is the price each package would need, if all of that product’s package prices moved by the same percent, for its NCR per year to match the leader’s. A code whose CMS billing unit isn’t an amount (such as “per dose”) is dosed in billing units. Comparisons are saved only in this browser.
